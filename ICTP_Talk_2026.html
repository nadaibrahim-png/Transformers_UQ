The Problem — Confidence ≠ Correctness

MOTIVATION 0:30 – 1:30
What goes wrong

Modern deep learning models are accurate but overconfident. A model that outputs "95% confident" should be correct 95% of the time. In practice, it often isn't.

This matters more in physics than in most domains. A miscalibrated classifier used in a neutrino oscillation analysis can systematically bias signal selection, inflating or suppressing discovery significance.

Overconfidence: P(correct | conf=0.95) << 0.95
Ideal: P(correct | conf=p) = p ∀p
Two distinct questions

Calibration

Are confidence scores numerically reliable? ECE measures the gap. Post-hoc fixes (TS, Isotonic) improve the numbers but add no new information.

Epistemic UQ ← our focus

Does the model know it doesn't know? Captures genuine model uncertainty — detects OOD, quantifies knowledge gaps, decomposable into aleatoric + epistemic.

Post-hoc calibration adjusts numbers after the fact.
Epistemic UQ models the uncertainty itself.

Why Uncertainty Matters in Neutrino Physics

MOTIVATION 1:30 – 2:30
The stakes
  • Neutrino oscillation experiments search for rare νᵉ appearance events in a large νμ background — signal purity is critical.
  • A classifier that is confidently wrong about νᵉ events passes background as signal — false discovery.
  • Experiments run for years; systematic biases introduced by miscalibrated models are hard to detect post-hoc.
  • OOD events (detector noise, beam fluctuations) should be flagged as uncertain — not confidently classified.
What we want from a UQ method

① Reliable confidence scores

Model says 80% → it should be right 80% of the time. Measured by ECE, NLL, Brier Score.

② OOD detection without OOD training data

High entropy on corrupted/anomalous events — even though the model never saw OOD examples during training.

③ Per-class fairness

Good overall ECE is not enough — the rare νᵉ signal class must also be well-calibrated.

Particle identification data from the MiniBooNE experiment at Fermilab — a Cherenkov radiation detector searching for νμ→νᵉ oscillations.

Each event is characterised by 50 reconstructed kinematic features: ring geometry, shower shape, particle momentum, timing. No manual feature engineering — raw detector observables.

Why FT-Transformer for tabular data?

Unlike TabTransformer (categorical only), FT-Transformer tokenises every numerical feature — giving each of the 50 kinematic observables its own learned embedding. Captures cross-feature attention patterns naturally.

CLS token — the aggregator

A learnable "listener" token. After 2 attention layers, its representation summarises all 50 feature tokens. Its final state feeds the binary classifier.

Residual connections + LayerNorm

output = LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x))
Gradient highway: ∂L/∂x always has a constant 1 term — prevents vanishing gradients across 2 encoder layers.

MC Dropout hook

Dropout (p=0.1) is placed after the FFN. At inference, model.enable_mc_dropout() keeps only this layer stochastic — enabling T=30 epistemic samples.

Learned attention weights — CLS token over 50 MiniBooNE features

Features F27, F23, F43 receive highest CLS attention. The roughly uniform distribution reflects MiniBooNE's 50 correlated kinematic observables — no single feature dominates.

Epistemic vs Aleatoric Uncertainty

UQ FRAMEWORK 5:30 – 6:30

Epistemic uncertainty

"The model doesn't know enough."

Caused by limited training data or unseen input regions. Reducible — more data or a better model makes it smaller. This is what MC Dropout, Ensembles, and SWAG capture.

Aleatoric uncertainty

"The data is inherently noisy."

Caused by overlapping class distributions or measurement noise. Irreducible — even a perfect model cannot eliminate it. Events near the νᵉ/νμ decision boundary always carry this.

Total uncertainty decomposition
H[p̄] = MI(y;θ|x) + Eβ[H[p|x,θ]] ⇧ total ⇧ epistemic ⇧ aleatoric

Deep Ensembles uniquely allow this decomposition via mutual information:

MI = H[p̄] − (1/M) Σₑ H[pₑ]

High MI on an event → the M models strongly disagree → epistemic uncertainty dominates → likely OOD or edge case.

This decomposition is only possible with ensemble-based methods — not with single-model approaches like temperature scaling.

🎯 This work targets epistemic uncertainty

MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles, and SWAG all estimate how much the model parameters contribute to uncertainty — the part reducible with more data. Temperature Scaling only calibrates confidence; it does not decompose or reduce epistemic uncertainty.

Method 1 — MC Dropout

UQ METHOD 6:30 – 8:00
Gal & Ghahramani 2016 — Dropout as Bayesian Approximation

Keep dropout active at inference. Each forward pass randomly silences neurons — drawing a different sub-network. T=30 passes produce T probability vectors whose mean and spread characterise uncertainty.

p̄ = (1/T) Σₑ softmax(fₚₛₘτ(x)) H = −Σₖ p̄ₖ log p̄ₖ ← predictive entropy
  • Cost: T=30 inference passes. ~30× slower than standard, but no retraining needed.
  • Posterior: Samples binary masks around a single MAP estimate — approximates one loss basin.
  • OOD: OOD inputs produce high-variance predictions → high H.
Live animation — T=30 stochastic passes

Each pass drops random neurons (grey). Mean p(νᵉ) stabilises as T increases.

Method 2 — Deep Ensembles

UQ METHOD 8:00 – 9:30
Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017 — NeurIPS

Train M=5 independent FT-Transformers from different random seeds. Each finds a different minimum in the loss landscape. At inference, average all M softmax outputs.

p̄ = (1/M) Σₑ pₑ(y|x) MI = H[p̄] − (1/M)Σₑ H[pₑ] ← epistemic
  • Diversity: Different random initialisations → different loss basins → genuine posterior diversity.
  • Decomposition: Mutual information separates epistemic from aleatoric — unique to ensembles.
  • Cost: M=5 full training runs. Most expensive method.
Loss landscape — 5 models find different minima

Each colour = one model. Different starting points → different minima → diverse predictions averaged at inference.

Method 3 — SWAG

UQ METHOD 9:30 – 11:00
Maddox et al. 2019 — Stochastic Weight Averaging-Gaussian

After standard training, continue with a high LR for 20 epochs. The weights wander around the loss basin floor. Collect snapshots θ₁…θṬ, fit a Gaussian:

θ̂_SWA = (1/T) Σ θₚ ← mean σ²_diag = Var(θₚ) ← diag var θ* = θ̂ + σ_diag ⊙ ε ← sample

Sample K=30 weight vectors at inference → K predictions → average → entropy.

Weight space: MAP → SWAG exploration → Gaussian sampling

Grey = MAP estimate. Orange = SWAG trajectory (high LR). Purple ellipse = fitted Gaussian. Dots = K=30 sampled weights.

Evaluation Metrics

METRICS 11:00 – 12:30

ECE — Expected Calibration Error

Weighted avg |accuracy − confidence| across 10 bins. Measures reliability of confidence scores. Lower = better calibrated.

Σ (|Bₙ|/n) · |acc(Bₙ)−conf(Bₙ)|

NLL — Negative Log-Likelihood

Proper scoring rule. Penalises confident wrong predictions exponentially. Sensitive to both calibration and sharpness.

−(1/n) Σ log p̂(yᵢ|xᵢ)

Brier Score

Mean squared error of probability forecasts. Proper scoring rule, bounded [0,1]. Combines calibration and resolution.

(1/n) Σ ||p̂ᵢ − yᵢ||²

Per-class ECE

ECE computed separately for νᵉ (signal) and νμ (background). Exposes class-specific miscalibration hidden by aggregate ECE.

Predictive Entropy (OOD)

H = −Σ p̄ₖ log p̄ₖ from MC/Ensemble/SWAG samples. High H → uncertain → OOD. Evaluated with AUROC vs noise-corrupted events.

Balanced Accuracy

Mean of per-class recall. Essential for 1:7 class imbalance. UQ methods don't change argmax predictions — this confirms accuracy is preserved.

Key Finding — Per-Class Calibration

KEY FINDING 14:00 – 15:30

Finding

Aggregate ECE hides per-class failures. The rare νᵉ signal class can be significantly worse-calibrated than the majority νμ class — invisible in the overall metric.

Epistemic UQ methods calibrate both classes via the same mechanism — more principled than a scalar post-hoc fix that may trade νᵉ calibration for νμ.

Why this matters
  • The νᵉ signal is exactly what the experiment is searching for — its calibration directly affects discovery sensitivity.
  • A method that looks best on aggregate ECE may actively worsen signal calibration.
  • Epistemic UQ methods (Ensemble, SWAG) treat both classes through the same uncertainty mechanism — more principled than scalar post-hoc fixes.
Always report per-class calibration when classes are imbalanced. Aggregate ECE is necessary but not sufficient.

Epistemic Uncertainty Decomposition

RESULTS 17:00 – 18:00

Deep Ensembles uniquely allow us to separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty via mutual information:

Total H[p̄] Epistemic MI = H[p̄] − (1/M)Σₑ H[pₑ] Aleatoric H[p̄] − MI
  • High epistemic, low aleatoric → OOD event. Model is uncertain because the input is unfamiliar.
  • Low epistemic, high aleatoric → boundary event. The models agree but the class is ambiguous.
  • Low both → confident, familiar in-distribution event.

Results on MiniBooNE test set (N=19,625)

Total H[p̄]median = 0.028 nats
Epistemic MImedian = 0.001 nats
Aleatoric E[H]median = 0.026 nats

∴ On IND data, most uncertainty is aleatoric (class overlap near the νᵉ/νμ boundary) — not epistemic. The model is well-trained; residual uncertainty is in the data itself.

Uncertainty decomposition — Deep Ensemble (M=5), test set

Total uncertainty decomposes cleanly: epistemic MI (red) is near-zero for familiar test events. The tail in aleatoric H (green) captures events near the signal/background boundary.

Conclusions

CONCLUSIONS 19:00 – 19:30
What we showed
  • FT-Transformer achieves ~93.4% balanced accuracy on MiniBooNE, matching gradient-boosted baselines.
  • All three epistemic UQ methods improve calibration over the standard baseline while also enabling OOD detection.
  • Aggregate ECE alone is insufficient — per-class ECE reveals calibration failures invisible in the overall metric.
  • Deep Ensembles uniquely decompose total uncertainty into epistemic + aleatoric via mutual information.
  • SWAG matches ensemble-level posterior quality at ~1× training cost — a practical middle ground.
Future work

Class-conditional calibration

Learn per-class temperature Tₖ to address the νᵉ vs νμ calibration trade-off directly.

Physical OOD scenarios

Replace Gaussian noise with realistic physics OOD: different beam conditions, detector ageing, new oscillation channels.

Multi-seed statistical analysis

Run ×5 seeds to report confidence intervals on ECE and balanced accuracy — more rigorous claim.
